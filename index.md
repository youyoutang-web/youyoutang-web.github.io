      </div>

      <p>
        My work asks how infrastructures make participation, access,
        care, and other social achievements possible—and
        <strong>where the coordination work required to sustain them is situated.</strong>
      </p>

    </div>


    <div class="hero-introduction">

      <p>
        When something does not work as expected, the system rarely stops.
        <strong>Someone adjusts.</strong>
      </p>

      <p>
        A person explains a confusing interface, rearranges their body,
        coordinates with others, develops a workaround, or maintains a routine.
        I study this work: <strong>what it makes possible, who performs it,
        and how infrastructures distribute it.</strong>
      </p>


      <p>
        My theoretical work develops
        <strong>Situated Cost Placement (SCP)</strong>,
        a theory of coordination placement that examines
        <strong>where coordination work is situated, what becomes dependent
        on that placement, and what capacities sustain it.</strong>
      </p>


      <details class="inline-detail">

        <summary>
          What is Situated Cost Placement?
        </summary>

        <div class="inline-detail-content">

          <p>
            SCP begins with a simple question:
            <strong>Where does the coordination work go?</strong>
          </p>

          <p>
            But placement is only the beginning. I ask where the work is
            situated within an infrastructural arrangement, what becomes
            dependent on that placement, and what allows the arrangement
            to continue.
          </p>

          <p>
            This means that coordination work is not automatically treated
            as a burden or as invisible labor. It may be
            <strong>routine, relational, creative, voluntary,
            institutionalized, or automated.</strong>
            What matters is its placement and what depends on it.
          </p>

          <p>
            <a href="/research/">Read more about SCP →</a>
          </p>

        </div>

      </details>


      <p>
        I am now following this question across different
        <strong>infrastructure settings</strong>.
        In cultural heritage, I explore how lightweight participation
        infrastructures can become part of everyday cultural spaces,
        enabling visitors to create and share traces of experience.
        In energy, I examine flexibility as a coordination achievement
        sustained across households, institutions, markets, and automated systems.
      </p>


      <details class="inline-detail">

        <summary>
          Why move across domains?
        </summary>

        <div class="inline-detail-content">

          <p>
            I am interested not only in applying SCP to different cases,
            but in testing <strong>how far the theory can travel</strong>.
          </p>

          <p>
            Cultural heritage and energy involve very different actors,
            temporalities, institutional arrangements, and forms of
            infrastructure. Bringing SCP into these settings allows me to
            examine what remains analytically useful, what changes,
            and where the theory reaches its limits.
          </p>

          <p>
            My current theoretical direction is therefore not to show that
            SCP “works everywhere,” but to ask:
            <strong>what travels, what transforms, and where does the
            mechanism stop explaining?</strong>
          </p>

        </div>

      </details>


      <p>
        Beyond research, I create spaces for exchange across

        I organize workshops and educational exchanges connecting

        regional opportunities, and emerging infrastructures.
      </p>
