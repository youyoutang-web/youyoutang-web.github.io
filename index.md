            <h3>
              Touch to Listen
            </h3>

            <p>
              A minimal NFC audio infrastructure for asynchronous care
              coordination at home.
            </p>

            <p>
              A four-week in-home deployment examining how physical placement,
              updating, and maintenance shape continued access.
            </p>

            <p>
              The system reduces interaction to a single physical action —
              tapping a tagged object to listen — and uses this constraint to
              examine where coordination work moves when interface operation
              is minimized.
            </p>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Empirical grounding
              </h4>

              <p>
                4-week in-home deployment<br>
                6 households · 10 older adults · 8 family members
              </p>

            </section>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Research focus
              </h4>

              <ul>
                <li>
                  Physical placement and routine alignment
                </li>
                <li>
                  Distributed configuration and maintenance
                </li>
                <li>
                  Asynchronous care coordination
                </li>
                <li>
                  Breakdown, reconfiguration, and continued access
                </li>
              </ul>

            </section>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Key observations
              </h4>

              <p>
                Reducing interface interaction did not eliminate coordination
                work. Instead, configuration, relocation, updating, and repair
                became more visible across household members, physical
                environments, and everyday routines.
              </p>

              <p>
                The study identified recurring patterns of stabilization,
                friction, delayed coordination, and unresolved drift, showing
                how continued access depends on whether coordination work can
                remain aligned over time.
              </p>

            </section>

            <h3>
              AI-mediated coordination
            </h3>

            <p>
              Current research examines how AI-mediated production, translation,
              communication, and cultural work reorganize coordination between
              people, computational systems, and institutional contexts.
            </p>

            <p>
              Rather than treating AI as a replacement for creative or
              organizational work, this research examines the coordination
              required to specify, screen, calibrate, translate, evaluate, and
              revise AI-mediated outputs.
            </p>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Research focus
              </h4>

              <ul>
                <li>
                  Human–AI specification and calibration
                </li>
                <li>
                  Screening and selection of generated content
                </li>
                <li>
                  Cultural judgment in multilingual media production
                </li>
                <li>
                  Translation, versioning, and revision
                </li>
                <li>
                  Coordination across human and computational actors
                </li>
              </ul>

            </section>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Current questions
              </h4>

              <p>
                What coordination work emerges when generation becomes easier
                but evaluation becomes more consequential?
              </p>

              <p>
                How are cultural judgments distributed between people,
                computational systems, and organizational workflows?
              </p>

              <p>
                What happens to coordination when the same content must be
                generated, translated, reviewed, and adapted across different
                linguistic and cultural contexts?
              </p>

            </section>

            </h3>

            <p>
              Ongoing research examining how cultural, spatial, and
              organizational arrangements are coordinated in changing urban
              environments.
            </p>

            <p>
              The research follows how local resources, organizations,
              businesses, researchers, artists, and public-facing projects
              become connected — and where the coordination required to sustain
              these connections is situated.
            </p>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Research focus
              </h4>

              <ul>
                <li>
                  Coordination across local actors and organizations
                </li>

                </li>
                <li>

                </li>
                <li>
                  Participation and cultural production
                </li>
                <li>

                </li>
              </ul>

            </section>


            <section class="research-subsection">

                Current observations
              </h4>

            <h3>
              Infrastructure coordination
            </h3>

            <p>
              An emerging research direction examining how coordination
              requirements are organized across large-scale infrastructure
              systems, and how different arrangements distribute coordination
              work among organizations, technologies, and operational actors.
            </p>

            <p>
              The research extends coordination analysis beyond everyday and
              cultural settings to systems where coordination depends on shared
              information, temporal alignment, distributed decision-making, and
              cross-organizational infrastructure.
            </p>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Research focus
              </h4>

              <ul>
                <li>
                  Coordination across organizational boundaries
                </li>
                <li>
                  Shared information and decision infrastructure
                </li>
                <li>
                  Temporal coordination and operational dependencies
                </li>
                <li>
                  Distributed responsibility and maintenance
                </li>
                <li>
                  How infrastructure enables, redistributes, or relocates
                  coordination work
                </li>
              </ul>

            </section>


            <section class="research-subsection">

              <h4>
                Current direction
              </h4>

              <p>
                Current work uses secondary evidence to examine how formal and
                institutional infrastructures organize coordination across
                complex systems.
              </p>

              <p>
                The research asks what coordination is required for a system to
                function, where that work is situated, what becomes dependent
                on its placement, and what capacities are needed to sustain
                coordination over time.
              </p>

              <p>
                The direction is being developed through comparative analysis,
                with future work extending toward interviews and collaboration
                with infrastructure and operational actors.
              </p>

            </section>
